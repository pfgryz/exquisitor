Editor Comments

"After careful consideration of the second-round reviews, we recommend
rejecting the manuscript. Although the authors have improved the
clarity and description of the proposed method, the revision does not
satisfactorily resolve several fundamental concerns raised during the
first review round. Reviewer 4 acknowledges the progress made and
considers the study design appropriate; however, they still identify
important gaps regarding the level of methodological detail, the
reproducibility of the ANN architecture, evaluation on imbalanced
datasets, and validation on larger and more diverse
benchmarks. Reviewer 3 maintains that core issues remain unresolved,
particularly the absence of benchmarking against state-of-the-art
sequence embedding tools such as DNABERT, the lack of a biologically
justified mutation model, and the omission of stronger baseline
methods and clearly defined reference datasets."
-Santiago Marco-Sola

Thank you for the opportunity to review your work. I'm sorry that we cannot be more positive on this occasion and hope you will not be deterred from submitting future work to BMC Bioinformatics.

Reviewer Comments:

Reviewer 4
Could the authors provide more details on the specific architecture and hyperparameters of the ANN used, as this information would aid reproducibility?

How does the proposed method perform on highly imbalanced datasets or rare taxa, and were any strategies used to address potential class imbalance?

Have the authors considered benchmarking Exquisitor on larger or more diverse real-world datasets to further validate its scalability and robustness beyond the presented benchmark?

Reviewer 3
While the authors provided clarifications, many major concerns remain
unresolved. Several key issues (benchmarking against state-of-the-art
tools such as DNABERT; redesign of the mutation model with biological
justification; inclusion of more advanced baseline methods like Gotoh
or MAFFT; and clearer reference datasets for benchmarking) were
deferred to future work. Since these points directly affect the rigor
and competitiveness of the method, the current manuscript still lacks
sufficient evidence of benefit over existing approaches. The work may
become suitable for consideration once these improvements are
implemented and adequately validated.

Reviewer 2
I appreciate that the authors have addressed the issues raised in the
previous review. However, a major concern remains: the proposed method
has not been adequately compared to existing approaches specifically
designed for metagenomic identification. Benchmarking against relevant
tools is essential when developing a bioinformatics method. While the
authors included comparisons with BLAST, Needleman–Wunsch alignment,
and k-mer embedding-based approaches, these algorithms were not
originally developed for metagenomic identification. As such, the
comparisons are not sufficiently convincing. To strengthen the
manuscript, the authors should provide more robust and targeted
evaluations demonstrating that their method outperforms existing tools
designed for the same purpose.
